Next, **Circuit Netlist** gives an HSPICE netlist of a dcdc converter.
The dcdc uses stages like the error amplifier, biaising transistors, comparator circuit and the output stage to regulate output voltage.
Suggest parameters namely : 

["l1", "w1", "l2", "w2", "l3", "w3", "l4", "w4", 
    "l5", "w5", "l6", "w6", "l7", "w7", "l8", "w8", 
    "l9", "w9", "l10", "w10", "l11", "w11", "l12", "w12", 
    "l13", "w13", "l14", "w14", "l15", "w15", "l16", "w16", 
    "l17", "w17", "l18", "w18", "l19", "w19", "l20", "w20", "l21", "w21", "l22", "w22"]

for the dcdc converter to achieve the desired performance on the following metrics:
output power (iq1): < 1.5e-5, low stability values = 0.0; and low output voltage difference values of < 0.005 V.

Stability is defined to be the voltage difference between consequtive time steps in the transient analysis.
The output voltage difference is the voltage difference between the output voltage and the reference voltage.

Transistors named as amp are involved in amplification, while transistors named cmp are part of the comparator and transistors named bias are involved in bias stage.

**Examples** gives demonstration of the BEST parameter settings to obtain the optimal target value and related metrics from HSPICE.
